# Alex, stage manager or show operator

<!-- Stories link to a section, for example persona-show-operator#goals. -->

## Goals

- Run a show from a cue list, a free palette, timecode or any mix of these.
- Know before doors open that the build, sources, outputs and links are ready.
- Recover from anything by pausing, jumping to a cue, or changing who drives a character.
- Reach a safe state with one control, with no agent or internet needed.
- Keep guests safe: mute, hide or remove anyone, and step in when a guest needs help.
- Rehearse cue to cue, even when performers are absent.

## Skills and tools

- Calling or running live shows, and reading a cue list.
- Lighting consoles, audio and cue software such as QLab and Ableton Live, and media servers.
- Works from a touchscreen or tablet, a physical control surface, or a keyboard and mouse.
- Does not use code or the command line, and does not need to know how the agents work.

## Context of use

At a console in a dark venue, under time pressure, with no chance to look anything up mid-show. The venue may have no internet. Live changes must be made by a person at the show and are logged. May be approving cues or live changes as a named delegate. On a solo show this role is covered by performer controls and rules set beforehand.

## Frustrations

- Show-control systems are rebuilt per production and differ from one show to the next.
- Each show has a different cue master and links between systems.
- Lighting has sometimes run separately with no link to the XR system **[assumed]**.
- Technical messages that do not say what to do next in the middle of a show **[assumed]**.
- Until the later health and output monitors arrive, a limited view of device and output state.

## Evidence

Discovery sections 6, 12 and 15 and the show control requirements. Stage manager and show operator are one persona here because both run the same controls. Split them if a real show shows that calling and operating need different tools. No stage manager has been interviewed.
