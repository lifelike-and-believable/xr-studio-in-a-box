# Keep guests clear of hazards and each other, and name an aftercare step

As Taylor, the venue attendant, I want to know how guests are kept clear of hazards and each other, be warned when a guest gets too close, and know the aftercare step so that I can help guests who are uncomfortable or confused.

## Acceptance criteria

- A show that lets guests walk declares how it keeps them clear of hazards, obstacles and each other, whether or not they wear anything, for example through its layout, lighting, signs and staff, from the boundaries and obstacles in the venue profile (story-venue-setup-venue-profile). The holder of the audience-facing safety and consent approval approves it with the show's safety rules. An agent can draft it but cannot approve it.
- Where guests wear headsets, the boundaries are also shown in the headset. When a guest comes within a distance the holder sets of a boundary or another guest, the guest sees a warning in the headset, and Taylor or Alex is told in text and with a sound which guest it is. Positions are used live only and never stored.
- Each show names an aftercare step: what happens if a guest is upset, who helps and where. The holder approves it with the show's safety rules. Guests see it in the pre-show (story-guest-care-notices-and-warnings), and Taylor can see it in plain words at any time during a session.
- Insurance and local venue rules for walking guests need review for each venue. Until the holder records that this review is done for the venue, a show with walking guests runs there only in tests and rehearsals with crew who know about it. An agent cannot record it.
- The story is tested in a room profiled for the test with the first release's test kit (story-build-and-install-measure-against-budget), with simulated guest positions, and once in a rehearsal with real guests in headsets.
