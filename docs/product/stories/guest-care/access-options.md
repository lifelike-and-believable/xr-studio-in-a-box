# Choose captions, audio description, a sign-interpreted view, seating or low motion

As Jamie, the audience member, I want to choose the access options the show offers, such as captions or a seated or low-motion option, so that I feel safe and comfortable during the show.

## Acceptance criteria

- Each show declares which access options it offers: captions for speech, audio description, a sign-interpreted view, a seated option and a low-motion option. It offers captions, a seated option and a low-motion option wherever its format allows, and where it does not, it says why in text. The seated and low-motion options, the warnings (story-guest-care-notices-and-warnings) and the pre-show time budget (story-guest-care-standard-pre-show) are the show's comfort options and limits. The director or their delegate declares them in production mode (story-show-safety-switch-modes). An agent cannot change them. This story defines the show's access options and comfort options. Other stories, such as story-interaction-design-guest-movement, refer to them by this story's id.
- Jamie chooses options in the pre-show and can change them during the show without leaving it.
- Captions of unscripted speech work in the venue with no internet, through local speech recognition or a person typing. Automatic captions are marked in text as an aid. The delay from speech to caption is measured and shown against a target the director or their delegate sets. Audio description plays in step with the scene it describes, within a delay the director or their delegate sets, and the test checks it over a full run. A sign-interpreted view shows the interpreter on each output that offers it, at a size the director or their delegate sets, within the same delay of the speech it interprets. Seated and low-motion options are checked by a scripted run in which every required action can be done seated, and no camera motion exceeds the limits the option declares. Sign language is always performed by a person, and never generated by AI.
- Before the first public show, Deaf and disabled artists, recruited and paid by the producer, review the access work and set the bar it must meet. Jordan records their findings in the project. The first public show cannot be accepted at the build for a public show gate (story-build-and-install-automatic-checks) until the review is recorded. This story can be accepted before the review.
- The story is tested in the Unreal Editor run as a standalone game on the budget file's test device (story-asset-checks-check-against-budget), with simulated guests choosing each option, and captions tested with the internet disconnected.
